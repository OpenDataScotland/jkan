notes: <p>Annual publication of mid-year population estimates by age and sex as at
  30 June every year, where estimates for higher geographies are aggregated from 2001
  Data Zones.<br/><br/>For population estimates for 2011 Data Zones, see <a href="http://statistics.gov.scot/data/population-estimates-current-geographic-boundaries">here</a><br/><br/>Data
  zones are the small area geography used by Scottish Government to allow statistics
  to be available across a number of policy areas. The data zone geography covers
  the whole of Scotland. Data zones were initially set up to nest within council area
  boundaries and to have a population of between 500 and 1,000 household residents.
  As much as possible, data zones were set up to contain households with similar social
  characteristics and to take into consideration physical boundaries. More information
  on data zone geography can be found on the <a href="http://www.gov.scot/Publications/2004/02/18917/33243">Scottish
  Government</a> website. <br/><br/>Following the 2011 Census the Scottish Government
  competed a consultation on the redrawing of data zone boundary and finalised the
  new data zones (2011 Data Zones) were published at the end of 2014.<br/><br/>Intermediate
  zones are built up from data zones. Intermediate zones are the statistical small
  geographical areas designed to contain a population of between 2,500 and 6,000 household
  residents. More information on intermediate geography can be found on the <a href="http://www.gov.scot/Publications/2005/02/20732/53081">Scottish
  Government</a> website.</p>
