notes: "<div>Introduced in Scotland from October 2000, the licensing of houses in\
  \ multiple occupation (HMO) is a crucial element within the private rented sector\
  \ regulatory framework, helping to improve both physical accommodation standards\
  \ and the management of tenancy issues. HMOs provide an important supply of housing,\
  \ particularly for groups of people such as students, transient workers, and those\
  \ who need extra support in their daily lives.</div>\n<div><br /></div>\n<div>Mandatory\
  \ HMO licensing applies to houses occupied by three or more persons, from three\
  \ or more families, as their only or main residence. All HMOs must meet physical\
  \ standards set by the licensing local authority under Part 5 of the Housing (Scotland)\
  \ Act 2006. Mandatory licensing applies to houses or flats occupied by three or\
  \ more unrelated people, who share bathroom or kitchen facilities. The owner of\
  \ an HMO must have a licence from the local authority where the property is situated.\
  \ Licensing helps ensure that accommodation is safe, well managed and of good quality.\
  \ HMOs are also covered by fire safety legislation.\_</div>\n<div><br /></div>\n\
  <div>Before granting a licence, the local authority must be satisfied that:</div>\n\
  <div><ul><li>the owner and any manager of the property is \u2018fit and proper\u2019\
  \ to hold a licence;</li><li>the property meets required physical standards; and</li><li>the\
  \ property is suitable for use as an HMO (or could be made so by including conditions\
  \ on the licence).</li></ul><div>This dataset provides an overview of licenses by\
  \ postcode, with additional Ward and Community Council information.</div></div>\n\
  <div><br /></div>\n<div>The local authority sets the standards required and also\
  \ sets the fees charged for a licence application.</div>"
