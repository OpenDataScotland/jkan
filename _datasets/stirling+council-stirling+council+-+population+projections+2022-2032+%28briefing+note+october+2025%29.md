notes: "<p>The <strong>National Records of Scotland (NRS)</strong> recently published\
  \ the results of its latest round of population projections for&nbsp;<br />sub-national\
  \ areas in Scotland. These are calculated using population estimates from a base\
  \ year, in this case <strong>2022</strong>. The figures are then rolled forward\
  \ on the basis of past trends in fertility, mortality and net migration for individual\
  \ council and health board areas to the year <strong>2047</strong>.</p>\n<p>NRS\
  \ assumes that past trends will continue, therefore they should not be seen as precise\
  \ forecasts of the future population.</p>\n<p>In many areas, including Stirling,\
  \ migration is the most important component of population change. It is also important\
  \ to note that projections become more uncertain the further ahead they go, especially\
  \ for smaller council areas like Stirling. With this in mind this briefing note\
  \ will focus on Stirling\u2019s population projection from <strong>2022 to 2032.</strong></p>\n\
  <p>The primary purpose of the national population projections is to provide an estimate\
  \ of the future population of Scotland as a common framework for use in national\
  \ planning in a number of different fields such as education and health. Projections\
  \ are also used for teacher workforce models and looking at the implications of\
  \ an ageing population. &nbsp;</p>"
