notes: '<p>The Scottish Government Urban Rural Classification provides a consistent
  way of defining urban and rural areas across Scotland. The classification is based
  upon two main criteria: (i) population as defined by the National Records of Scotland,
  and (ii) accessibility based on drive time analysis to differentiate between accessible
  and remote areas in Scotland.<br/><br/>The classification distinguishes between
  urban, rural and remote areas within Scotland and includes the following categories:
  <br/><br/>1 - Large  Urban Areas Settlements of over 125,000 people; <br/><br/>2
  - Other Urban Areas Settlements of 10,000 to 125,000 people; <br/><br/>3 - Accessible
  Small Towns Settlements of between 3,000 and 10,000 people and within 30 minutes
  drive of a settlement of 10,000 or more;<br/><br/>4 - Remote Small Towns Settlements
  of between 3,000 and 10,000 people and with a drive time of over 30 minutes to a
  settlement of 10,000 or more; <br/><br/>5 - Accessible Rural Settlements of less
  than 3,000 people and within 30 minutes drive of a settlement of 10,000 or more;
  <br/><br/>6 - Remote Rural Settlements of less than 3,000 people and with a drive
  time of over 30 minutes to a settlement of 10,000 or more.<br/><br/>Starting with
  the 2016 version of the classification the name format was changed to a single year.
  The single year refers to the date of the mid-year estimates on which the population
  statistics for the Settlements are based. The change to the name format was made
  in order to make it clearer in the title of the classification the date of the statistics
  used.<br/><br/>Further information can be found on the <a href="https://www.gov.scot/collections/agriculture-fisheries-and-rural-statistics/#urbanruralclassification">Urban
  Rural Classification</a> section of the Scottish Government website .</p>'
