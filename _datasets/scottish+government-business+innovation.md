notes: '<p>Information on the percentage of businesses involved in innovation activity
  from the UK Innovation Survey (UKIS) by type of innovation activity and number of
  employees.<br/><br/>The UK definition of innovation is based on an Organisation
  for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) definition, outlined in the <a
  href="https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oslo-manual-2018_9789264304604-en.html">Oslo
  Manual 2018</a>. This definition includes any of the following activities, if they
  occurred during the survey period:<br/><br/>[1] The introduction of a new or improved
  product (goods or services).<br/><br/>[2] Business processes used to produce or
  supply all goods or services that the business has introduced, regardless of their
  origin. These innovations may be new to business or new to the market.<br/><br/>[3]
  Engagement in innovation projects not yet complete or abandoned.<br/><br/>[4] Investment
  activities in areas such as internal research and development, training, acquisition
  of external knowledge or machinery and equipment linked to innovation activities.<br/><br/><br/>This
  definition is then used to define the measures of innovation activity as follows:<br/><br/>Innovation
  active - a business that had engaged in any of the activities described in points
  [1] to [3] above.<br/><br/>Broader innovator - a business that had engaged in any
  of the activities described in points [1] to [4] above.<br/><br/>Product innovator
  - a business that undertakes activity [1] from above is described as a ''product
  innovator''. That is a business that brings to the market or into use by the business,
  new and improved products, including both tangible goods and the provision of services.<br/><br/>Business
  process innovator - a business that undertakes activity [2] from the definition
  of innovation above is described as a ''business process innovator''. This definition
  was introduced in the 2023 UKIS (survey period 2020 to 2022). It is broader than
  the process innovator definition in previous UKIS surveys and is therefore not comparable.
  It aligns with the OECD''s revised approach in conceptualising ''innovation''.<br/><br/>To
  provide an approximate time series for business process innovators, we have made
  the assumption that business process innovators in UKIS 2023 are roughly equivalent
  to businesses in previous surveys that were either ''Process innovators'' or ''Wider
  innovators''. More information on this can be found in our <a href="https://www.gov.scot/publications/uk-innovation-survey-2023-results-for-scotland/">UK
  Innovation Survey - Results for Scotland 2023</a> publication. Caution is advised
  when looking at the change between 2020 to 2022 and earlier survey periods.<br/><br/>As
  a result of the above changes, ''Wider innovation active businesses'' and ''Process
  innovator'' are no longer included as categories of ''Innovation Activity'' in the
  data. In addition, the combined ''Product AND process innovation'' and ''Product
  AND/OR process innovation'' categories are no longer included. Instead, the data
  provides information on businesses that are ''Business process AND product innovators''
  and ''Business process AND/OR product innovators''.<br/><br/>Innovation related
  activity - a business that undertakes activity [4] from the definition of innovation
  above is described as a ''Business engaged in an innovation related activity'',
  which includes categories of innovation directed investment such as: R&amp;D (research
  and development), capital goods and software acquisition, design activity, for implementing
  current innovations or directed to future product or process changes. <br/><br/>Users
  should note that ''Scaled back innovation'' was a new option introduced in UKIS
  2017 (2014 to 2016 reporting period). It was removed as an option in UKIS 2023 (2020
  to 2022 reporting period).<br/><br/>Geographic locations of businesses are based
  on businesses'' head office location. Business size, given by number of employees,
  is the total number of employees anywhere in the UK, irrespective of head office
  location. For example, a business in Scotland with 250 employees may have some based
  at their head office in Scotland and others based elsewhere in the UK.<br/><br/>An
  asterisk (*) indicates that a value has been withheld from publication to maintain
  confidentiality. A hyphen (-) indicates that the value is not available (for example,
  ''Scaled back innovation'' was only an option for the 2014-2016 to 2018-2020 survey
  reporting periods, therefore is unavailable for other survey periods).<br/><br/>The
  UKIS is the main data source for business innovation in the UK. The survey is conducted
  every 2 years and asks businesses with 10 or more employees from Standard Industrial
  Classification 2007 (SIC 2007) sectors B to N for information on their innovation
  activities over the preceding three-year period. The most recent survey is the UKIS
  2023, which covered innovation activity in the period 2020 to 2022. If information
  was not available for calendar years, businesses were asked that their return covered
  the nearest financial years. The three year reporting period is consistent with
  previous UKIS surveys and is widely used internationally. The survey was voluntary
  and was conducted primarily through an electronic questionnaire. Businesses that
  did not complete an electronic response were contacted for a telephone interview.
  Prior to the UKIS 2017 (reporting period 2014 to 2016), the survey was conducted
  through both a postal questionnaire and telephone interview for businesses that
  had not yet completed a postal response.<br/><br/>The UKIS 2023 sampled 32,273 UK
  businesses, with responses received from 14,570, giving a response rate of 45.1%.
  1,547 businesses based in Scotland responded, giving a Scottish response rate of
  45.7%. Generally, around 1,500 businesses based in Scotland respond to the survey.<br/><br/>Survey
  results are based on weighted data in order to be representative of the population
  of businesses (with 10 or more employees from SIC 2007 sectors B to N).<br/><br/>The
  coronavirus (COVID-19) pandemic began in the first quarter of 2020 and is likely
  to have had an impact on business innovation in the three year period businesses
  were reporting on in the UKIS 2023, as well as the latter part of the period covered
  by the previous UKIS (UKIS 2021, reporting period 2018 to 2020). High inflation
  and energy prices may also have had an impact in the latest survey.<br/><br/>The
  UKIS 2023 was funded and developed by the UK Government Department for Business
  and Trade (DBT). It was administered by the Office for National Statistics (ONS)
  on behalf of DBT and the UK Government Department for Science, Innovation and Technology
  (DSIT).<br/><br/>More information is available from our <a href="https://www.gov.scot/publications/uk-innovation-survey-2023-results-for-scotland/">UK
  Innovation Survey - Results for Scotland 2023</a> publication and the UK Government''s
  <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/uk-innovation-survey-2023-report/united-kingdom-innovation-survey-2023-report">United
  Kingdom Innovation Survey 2023: Report</a>.</p>'
