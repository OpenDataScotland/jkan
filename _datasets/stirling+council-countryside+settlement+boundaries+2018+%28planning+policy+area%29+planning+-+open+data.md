notes: "<div>A Countryside Policy Boundary (CPB) is a planning designation used by\
  \ local authorities to define the limits of built-up areas and control development\
  \ in the countryside. It is typically drawn around settlements to distinguish between\
  \ urban or village areas where development is generally acceptable and rural areas\
  \ where stricter planning controls apply.</div>\n<div><br /></div>\n<div><b>Purpose\
  \ of a Countryside Policy Boundary</b></div>\n<div><ul><li>Prevent Urban Sprawl\
  \ \u2013 Helps manage the spread of settlements and protects rural areas from unplanned\
  \ development.</li><li>Protect the Countryside \u2013 Ensures open spaces, landscapes,\
  \ and agricultural land are safeguarded from inappropriate development.</li><li>Focus\
  \ Development in Sustainable Locations \u2013 Encourages new housing, infrastructure,\
  \ and businesses within defined settlement areas rather than scattered rural development.</li><li>Support\
  \ Rural Character \u2013 Maintains the distinct identity of rural communities by\
  \ preventing settlements from merging.</li></ul></div>\n<div><b>Development Within\
  \ &amp; Outside CPBs</b></div>\n<div><ul><li>Inside the Boundary: Development is\
  \ generally supported if it aligns with the Local Plan (e.g., housing, businesses,\
  \ and infrastructure).</li><li>Outside the Boundary: Strict planning controls apply,\
  \ meaning new development is usually restricted unless it serves rural purposes,\
  \ such as agriculture, forestry, or essential infrastructure. Some exceptions may\
  \ include rural exception housing, tourism-related development, or re-use of existing\
  \ buildings.</li></ul></div>\n<div><b>Difference Between CPB and Green Belt</b></div>\n\
  <div><ul><li>A CPB is a local planning tool specific to certain settlements, whereas\
  \ the Green Belt is a national designation aimed at preventing urban expansion over\
  \ a much wider area.</li></ul></div>"
