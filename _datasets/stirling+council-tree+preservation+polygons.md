notes: '<p>A TPO is made by the Local Authority, under Section 160 of the Town and
  Country Planning (Scotland) Act 1997, and within the procedures set out in the Town
  and Country Planning (Tree Preservation Order and Trees in Conservation Areas) (Scotland)
  Regulations 2010. They are made to protect individual trees, groups of trees or
  woodlands which have particular amenity value, make a significant contribution to
  the landscape or townscape or because there may be a potential threat to the trees.</p>

  <p>&nbsp;In deciding which trees qualify to become protected the local authority
  must ensure that the trees contribute to the amenity and attractiveness of an area
  and be under threat in some way. Either individual specimens or groups can be protected
  in a single Order.&nbsp;</p>

  <p>More information and guidance on Tree Preservation orders and Trees in conservation
  can be found in Planning Circular 1/2011. Which provides an overview of the TPO
  procedures, explaining how the requirements from the Act and Regulations fit together.</p>

  <p>This dataset was published September 2025 and contains an updated schema. Each
  dataset contains information regarding;</p>

  <ul><li>Location and calculated postcode</li><li>Order Reference and Order Number</li><li>Issuing
  authority (given historic nature of the orders)</li><li>Year and Month of creation</li><li>Order
  type - individual, group, or woodland</li><li>Tree identifiers were applicable (labels)</li><li>Tree
  species information were available</li><li>Status (which may include protection
  areas and trees flagged as removed)</li><li>Conservation area</li><li>Community
  Council, Ward, and Datazone details.</li><li>Comments</li></ul>

  <p><br />Note: positional information may include some degree of inaccuracy, especially
  regarding boundaries and area coverage.</p>'
