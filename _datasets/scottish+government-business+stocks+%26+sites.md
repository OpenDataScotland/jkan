notes: "<p>Estimates about the number of VAT/PAYE registered businesses (stocks) and\
  \ sites operating in Scotland, broken down by various groupings including industry\
  \ sector, business size, local authority area, and intermediate zone areas are produced\
  \ by Scottish Government within the Businesses in Scotland publication.  The Businesses\
  \ in Scotland publication data are largely sourced from the Inter-Departmental Business\
  \ Register (IDBR).  The IDBR is maintained by the Office for National Statistics\
  \ (ONS) and is a database of all registered businesses operating in the UK i.e.\
  \ businesses that are registered for VAT and/or PAYE.<br/><br/>Statistics from the\
  \ Businesses in Scotland publication are used to provide insight into the characteristics\
  \ of Scotland's business stock, and how Scotland's business stock has changed over\
  \ time.<br/><br/>The estimates also include information about business employment\
  \ and turnover (\xA3 millions). The function of these data is to act as 'auxiliary\
  \ variables' that can be used to (i) classify businesses by employee/turnover size\
  \ band and (ii) calculate shares of employment and turnover across industrial sectors,\
  \ local areas, business sizes etc. The nature of the underlying processes used to\
  \ update these variables on the IDBR means that direct comparisons made using the\
  \ absolute values are less reliable. Note the estimates do not include turnover\
  \ information for financial and insurance businesses as it is not available on a\
  \ comparable basis.<br/><br/>A business (business stock) can be defined as the smallest\
  \ combination of legal units (generally based on VAT and/or PAYE records) that is\
  \ an organisational unit producing goods or services, which benefits from a certain\
  \ degree of autonomy in decision-making, especially for the allocation of its current\
  \ resources. Note a business carries out one or more activities at one or more locations.\
  \ Businesses are counted only once in Scotland-level estimates or once each in each\
  \ of the geographical areas.<br/><br/>A local unit (business site) is a business\
  \ or part thereof (e.g. a workshop, factory, warehouse, office, mine or depot) situated\
  \ in a geographically identified place.<br/><br/>The estimates also include unregistered\
  \ businesses. A large number of very small businesses are not VAT/PAYE registered\
  \ because they are small sole traders/partnerships with no employees and an annual\
  \ turnover of less than the VAT threshold. The estimates for unregistered businesses\
  \ are estimated primarily using data from the Labour Force Survey, which is subject\
  \ to sampling error - this should be considered when making year on year comparisons\
  \ between the number of unregistered businesses.<br/><br/>The estimates also include\
  \ two National Indicators: 'The number of businesses' and 'High growth businesses'.\
  \ The registered business stock rate, the total stock of private sector registered\
  \ businesses per 10,000 resident adults in Scotland, is the basis of the Scotland\
  \ Performs National Indicator: 'The number of businesses'. The high growth business\
  \ stock count is the basis of the Scotland Performs National Indicator: 'High growth\
  \ businesses'. We defined a high growth business as a business with 10+ employees\
  \ in the base year (x - 3) exhibiting an average of 20% growth over three years\
  \ in terms of turnover.  In practice, average annualised growth of 20% per annum\
  \ over three years would be equal to 72.8% growth from year x - 3 to year x.<br/><br/>More\
  \ detailed information can be found on the <a href=\"https://www.gov.scot/publications/businesses-in-scotland-2024/\"\
  >Businesses in Scotland</a> website.<br/><br/>The population estimates used to form\
  \ the rates are sourced from the <a href=\"https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/publications/mid-2024-population-estimates/#\"\
  >Mid-Year Population Estimates</a>, published by the National Records of Scotland.\
  \  The term 'per 10,000 adults' is defined as people aged 16 or over resident in\
  \ Scotland.<br/></p>"
