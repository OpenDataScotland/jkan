notes: "<div>What is Landscape Character Assessment?</div>\n<div><br /></div>\n<div>Landscape\
  \ Character Assessment (LCA) is the process of identifying and describing variation\
  \ in the character of the landscape. LCAs identify and explain the combination of\
  \ elements and features that make landscapes distinct from one another by mapping\
  \ and describing Landscape Character Types and Areas. The associated description\
  \ of their distinctive characteristics shows how the landscape is perceived and\
  \ experienced by people.</div>\n<div><br /></div>\n<div>LCA analyses in detail the\
  \ three main physical landscape components of:</div>\n<div><ul><li>landform (e.g.\
  \ hills, straths, glens) \u2013 such as, are hills rounded or angular?</li><li>land\
  \ cover (e.g. rivers, lochs, woodland, farmland) \u2013 such as, what is the dominant\
  \ type and pattern of woodland?</li><li>settlement (e.g. towns, villages, farmsteads)\
  \ \u2013 such as, what is the pattern of settlement?</li></ul></div>\n<div>It then\
  \ looks at how all these combine to form the landscapes we see and experience. Areas\
  \ with similar patterns of components are mapped together as a particular Landscape\
  \ Character Type. These can occur in more than one location, for example, \u201C\
  Rugged Massif\u201D occurs in all different areas of Scotland, such as Inverness\
  \ District and Skye &amp; Lochalsh; and \u201CRocky Moorland\u201D\_ in Lochaber,\
  \ Argyll and the Outer Hebrides.</div>\n<div><br /></div>\n<div>LCA provides baseline\
  \ information \u2013 including a shared written understanding of the key characteristics\
  \ of a landscape \u2013 that can be used to guide landscape change. For example,\
  \ such information may inform:</div>\n<div><ul><li>development plans</li><li>sensitivity\
  \ studies</li><li>decisions on development proposals</li><li>land management plans</li><li>forestry\
  \ and woodland strategies</li><li>agri-environment schemes</li><li>landscape designations</li></ul></div>\n\
  <div>LCA is also the baseline for, and informs the use of, many landscape tools\
  \ and techniques, such as Landscape and Visual Impact Assessment (LVIA).</div>"
